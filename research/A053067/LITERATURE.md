# Literature map for the A053067 congruence

This note records literature that is directly relevant to the fixed-width
divisibility condition for A053067,

\[
q^n X_n(q)\equiv Y_n(q)\pmod p,
\]

or equivalently

\[
(q-1)A(n)=X_n(q)R_n(q)-n.
\]

It is a research map, not a claim that any cited theorem already resolves the
OEIS problem.

## 1. Exponential congruences

**A. Balog, K. A. Broughan, I. E. Shparlinski,
"On the Number of Solutions of Exponential Congruences",
Acta Arith. 148 (2011), 93--103.**
arXiv:1003.1997, DOI 10.4064/aa148-1-7.

They study congruences such as

\[
x^x\equiv a\pmod p
\]

and obtain nontrivial bounds for the number of solutions.  Their methods split
according to multiplicative order and use additive combinatorics and sparse
polynomial estimates.

**Relevance here.**  Our congruence is different because the exponential base
is fixed, while the right-hand side is a rational function of the index.
Nevertheless the same organizing principle is useful: small multiplicative
order should be treated algebraically and large order statistically.

The A053067 computations now strongly support exactly this split:

- small-order factors are caught by explicit congruence families;
- large-order factors behave with density close to random divisibility.

## 2. Character sums over multiplicative subgroups

**J. Bourgain, A. A. Glibichuk, S. V. Konyagin,
"Estimates for the Number of Sums and Products and for Exponential Sums in
Fields of Prime Order",
J. London Math. Soc. 73 (2006), 380--398.**
DOI 10.1112/S0024610706022721.

**M.-C. Chang, I. E. Shparlinski,
"Double Character Sums over Subgroups and Intervals",
Bull. Aust. Math. Soc. 90 (2014), 376--384.**
arXiv:1401.6611.

**K. Gong,
"An elementary approach to character sums over multiplicative subgroups".**
arXiv:1401.4618.

These papers develop nontrivial cancellation estimates for character sums in
which one or more variables range over multiplicative subgroups of
\(\mathbb F_p^*\).

**Direct connection to A053067.**  For fixed width \(d\), prime \(p\), and

\[
H=\langle 10^d\rangle,
\]

the exact number of zero classes is

\[
Z_{p,d}
=
|H|-1+
\sum_{t\in H}\chi(\Delta_q(t)),
\]

where \(\chi\) is the quadratic character and

\[
\Delta_q(t)
=
(q-1)\bigl(-(7q+1)t^2+(18q-2)t-(7q+1)\bigr).
\]

Thus the density question for one prime is literally a quadratic-character
sum over a multiplicative subgroup.

A simple Weil-bound argument after parametrizing the subgroup already gives

\[
Z_{p,d}=|H|-1+O(\sqrt p).
\]

The deeper subgroup-character-sum literature is relevant if we later need
stronger uniformity in ranges where \(|H|\) is smaller than \(\sqrt p\).

## 3. Polynomial values in multiplicative subgroups

**I. E. Shparlinski,
"Polynomial Values in Small Subgroups of Finite Fields",
Rev. Mat. Iberoam. 32 (2016), 1127--1136.**
arXiv:1401.0964, DOI 10.4171/RMI/909.

This studies when polynomial values can remain inside a small multiplicative
subgroup.

**Relevance here.**  The condition

\[
q^n=\frac{Y_n(q)}{X_n(q)}\pmod p
\]

requires a rational function of \(n\) to land in the subgroup generated by
\(q\), and moreover to land at the exponent position indexed by \(n\).
The first part is directly in the orbit of this literature; the second is the
extra discrete-log synchronization that makes A053067 harder.

## 4. Generalized exponential polynomials and recurrences

**A. J. van der Poorten, I. E. Shparlinski,
"On linear recurrence sequences with polynomial coefficients",
Glasgow Math. J. 38 (1996), 147--155.**
DOI 10.1017/S0017089500031372.

**G. R. Everest, I. E. Shparlinski,
"Divisor Sums of Generalised Exponential Polynomials",
Canad. Math. Bull. 39 (1996), 35--46.**
DOI 10.4153/CMB-1996-005-5.

For a fixed decimal width \(d\), the numerator of the A053067 closed form is
an exponential polynomial in \(n\): a polynomial in \(n\) times \(q^n\)
minus another polynomial in \(n\).

**Relevance here.**  This is the correct general structural class for a
fixed-width slice of A053067.  The literature gives broad results on values,
divisors, and recurrence structure, but does not appear to contain a theorem
forcing every term of our particular exponential polynomial to be composite.

## 5. GCD results for recurrence / S-unit sequences

The line of work of Bugeaud--Corvaja--Zannier, Fuchs, Luca, and others proves
that gcds of multiplicatively independent exponential or recurrence
expressions are usually small.  A convenient survey reference is the
literature on gcds of linear recurrences; a representative theorem states
that gcds of expressions of the form

\[
F_1(n)a^n+G_1(n),\qquad F_2(n)b^n+G_2(n)
\]

are subexponential outside exceptional algebraic dependence.

**Relevance here.**  These results point in the same direction as the exact
identity already proved on the branch:

\[
p\mid A(n),\ p\mid R_n(q)\Longrightarrow p\mid n.
\]

They make a proof based on sharing a large cyclotomic factor with
\(q^n-1\) even less plausible.

## 6. Covering systems and Sierpinski/Riesel methods

The classical Sierpinski/Riesel strategy uses a finite set of primes whose
zero residue classes cover every exponent.  Filaseta, Finch, and Kozek,
"On powers associated with Sierpinski numbers, Riesel numbers and Polignac's
conjecture", J. Number Theory 128 (2008), 1916--1940, is a representative
modern reference.

**Relevance here.**  A053067 admits an analogous finite-cover formulation,
and the CEGAR/Z3 tooling on this branch implements it.  However the new
character-sum formula shows why the analogy is weak for large-order factors:
a typical prime kills only about \(1/p\) of indices, rather than producing a
large specially engineered residue family.

## 7. Prime-value heuristic for consecutive concatenations

OEIS A244424 records the standard heuristic for concatenating consecutive
integers: a concatenation of length \(k\) has size roughly
\(10^{k\log_{10}k}\), so the expected prime contribution is of order

\[
\frac1{k\log k},
\]

whose sum diverges like \(\log\log k\).

The same calculation applies to A053067 more specifically.  Around index
\(n\), the concatenated integers have size about \(n^2/2\), so

\[
\log A(n)\sim 2n\log n.
\]

After accounting for the exact local exclusions by 2, 3 and 5, the crude
unconditional prime hazard is of order

\[
\frac{0.375}{n\log n}.
\]

The sum still diverges.  This is not a proof, but it is important strategic
evidence: the natural random model predicts infinitely many prime terms,
not eventual compositeness, while also predicting that the next prime can be
extremely far away because \(\log\log n\) grows so slowly.

## 8. Period-corrected prime heuristics for exponential sequences

**J. Grantham, A. Granville,
"Fibonacci primes, primes of the form \(2^n-k\) and beyond",
J. Number Theory 261 (2024), 190--219.**
arXiv:2307.07894, DOI 10.1016/j.jnt.2024.02.002.

They develop prime-value heuristics for exponentially growing recurrence
sequences and emphasize a point directly relevant to A053067: the local Euler
factors should be organized by the period of the sequence modulo a prime,
rather than merely by prime size.

For fixed decimal width \(d\), A053067 now has exactly the local datum needed
for an analogous calculation:

\[
\rho_{p,d}
=
\frac{Z_{p,d}}{p\,\operatorname{ord}_p(10^d)},
\]

where \(Z_{p,d}\) is the exact number of zero classes in the full period and

\[
Z_{p,d}
=
r-1+
\sum_{t\in\langle10^d\rangle}\chi(\Delta_q(t)).
\]

Thus a refined A053067 prime heuristic can be built from exact local
divisibility densities instead of assuming independent random divisibility.

This is a promising computational/theoretical project in its own right:
estimate the local correction product for successive width classes and compare
its prediction with the actual small-factor survival rates.

## Current interpretation

The literature and our computations now line up:

1. **small multiplicative order** -> explicit congruence families;
2. **large multiplicative order** -> zero density close to \(1/p\), governed
   by character sums over subgroups;
3. **cyclotomic/common-repunit factors** -> strongly restricted and not a
   plausible universal mechanism;
4. **finite covering by small primes** -> empirically weak and theoretically
   increasingly implausible;
5. **prime occurrence** -> heuristic remains divergent, so a later prime is
   still the more natural probabilistic expectation.

The proof program should therefore focus on rigorous structural statements
about the zero-class density and on ruling out classes of universal covering
mechanisms, while the computation program searches for an actual later prime.
