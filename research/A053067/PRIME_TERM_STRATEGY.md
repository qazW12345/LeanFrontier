# Prime-term strategy for A053067

This document records the strongest current route toward the conjecture that
A053067 contains infinitely many prime terms.

The purpose is to separate proved statements from heuristic and genuinely open
steps.

## Target conjecture

Let \(A(n)\) be OEIS A053067.  The working conjecture is

\[
\boxed{
\#\{n\le N:A(n)\text{ is prime}\}\to\infty.
}
\]

The period-corrected heuristic suggests much more specifically that

\[
\#\{n\le N:A(n)\text{ is prime}\}
\]

should grow on the scale of \(\log\log N\), with a coefficient determined by
the decimal-width singular corrections \(C_d\).

## Proven layer 1: exact fixed-width algebra

For a genuine fixed-width block with \(q=10^d\),

\[
(q-1)A(n)=X_n(q)R_n(q)-n,
\]

where

\[
X_n(q)=(q-1)L_n+1,
\qquad
R_n(q)=1+q+\cdots+q^{n-1}.
\]

This gives exact periodic divisor families modulo every prime.

## Proven layer 2: no finite local obstruction

For every modulus \(m\ge1\), there are infinitely many genuine fixed-width
indices with

\[
\boxed{A(n)\equiv1\pmod m.}
\]

Consequences:

- no finite set of primes divides every sufficiently large term;
- infinitely many distinct primes divide A053067 terms;
- an infinite pairwise-coprime subsequence exists.

Thus a Sierpinski/Riesel finite covering cannot explain eventual
compositeness.

## Proven layer 3: no polynomial-factorization obstruction

Define

\[
P_n(x)=
L_nx^{n-1}+(L_n+1)x^{n-2}+\cdots+U_n.
\]

For every \(n\ge3\), Kakuma's 2026 irreducibility criterion applies and gives

\[
\boxed{P_n(x)\text{ irreducible in }\mathbb Z[x].}
\]

Thus systematic compositeness cannot come from a nontrivial factorization of
the coefficient polynomial.

## Proven layer 4: exact local zero density

For a prime \(p>5\), fixed width \(d\), \(q=10^d\), and
\(r=\operatorname{ord}_p(q)\), divisibility by \(p\) reduces, after fixing
\(n\bmod r\), to a quadratic congruence in \(n\bmod p\).

The zero count is a quadratic-character sum over the subgroup generated by
\(q\).  Away from finitely many fixed-width degeneracies,

\[
\rho_{p,d}
=
\frac1p+
O\!\left(\frac1{r\sqrt p}\right).
\]

This explains why large-order prime divisibility is almost random.

## Proven layer 5: positive fixed-width singular factor

For every fixed decimal width \(d\),

\[
C_d
=
\prod_{p>5}
\frac{1-\rho_{p,d}}{1-1/p}
\]

converges to a finite positive constant:

\[
\boxed{0<C_d<\infty.}
\]

The proof combines the character-sum estimate with Pappalardi's estimate for
reciprocals of multiplicative orders.

Thus no fixed decimal width has a vanishing local singular series.

## Proven layer 6: every finite prime cutoff has divergent width mass

For a finite prime bound \(B\),

\[
C_d(B)
=
\prod_{5<p\le B}
\frac{1-\rho_{p,d}}{1-1/p}
\]

is a positive periodic function of \(d\).

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{d\le D}\frac{C_d(B)}d
=
\mu_B\log D+O_B(1),
\qquad \mu_B>0.
}
\]

Hence no finite collection of local prime effects can make the predicted
total prime mass finite.

## Numerical layer

At \(B=1000\), the exact correction was computed for every width
\(1\le d\le500\):

\[
\min C_d(B)=0.56348,\qquad
\max C_d(B)=1.40487,
\]

with arithmetic mean \(1.05101\), geometric mean \(1.03588\), and
harmonic-weighted mean \(1.06370\).

The measured harmonic mass is

\[
\sum_{d\le500}\frac{C_d(1000)}d=7.22555,
\]

compared with the uncorrected

\[
\sum_{d\le500}\frac1d=6.79282.
\]

A larger width sweep is in progress.

## Heuristic prime mass per width

A term in a \(d\)-digit fixed-width band has

\[
\log A(n)\sim dn\log10.
\]

After the exact elementary \(2,3,5\) filter and the singular correction, the
expected number of prime terms contributed by one full width band is

\[
\boxed{
E_d\sim\frac{3}{8}\frac{C_d}{d}.
}
\]

Therefore the refined prime heuristic predicts infinitely many prime terms if

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{d\ge1}\frac{C_d}{d}=\infty.
}
\]

## Proven layer 7: prime widths have a uniform growing-cutoff correction

Restrict the decimal width \(D\) itself to primes.

For every local prime \(p<D\),

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(10^D)=\operatorname{ord}_p(10),
\]

because \(\operatorname{ord}_p(10)\mid p-1<D\) and therefore cannot be
divisible by the prime \(D\).

The pointwise character-sum estimate then gives

\[
\left|
\rho_{p,D}-\frac1p
\right|
\ll
\frac1{\operatorname{ord}_p(10)\sqrt p},
\]

uniformly in the prime width \(D\), for all \(p<D\).

Pappalardi's reciprocal-order estimate implies

\[
\sum_p
\frac1{\operatorname{ord}_p(10)\sqrt p}<\infty.
\]

Hence the naturally growing truncated correction

\[
C_D^{<D}
=
\prod_{5<p<D}
\frac{1-\rho_{p,D}}{1-1/p}
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{
0<c\le C_D^{<D}\le C<\infty
}
\]

uniformly over all sufficiently large prime decimal widths \(D\).

Since

\[
\sum_{D\ {\rm prime}}\frac1D=\infty,
\]

we obtain the rigorous local-density statement

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{D\ {\rm prime}}
\frac{C_D^{<D}}D=\infty.
}
\]

Thus even when the local prime cutoff grows linearly with the decimal width,
the corrected prime mass remains divergent on the thin subsequence of prime
widths alone.

The only local singular-series contribution not covered by this theorem is
the tail \(p\ge D\), where order collapse can occur only if

\[
D\mid\operatorname{ord}_p(10),
\]

hence only for primes \(p\equiv1\pmod D\).

This reduces the width-uniform local problem to a sparse arithmetic-progression
tail.

## Analytic gap A: width-uniform singular mass

We know \(C_d>0\) for each fixed \(d\), but pointwise positivity alone does not
imply

\[
\sum_d C_d/d=\infty.
\]

The next analytic target is an average lower bound such as

\[
\liminf_{D\to\infty}
\frac{\sum_{d\le D}C_d/d}{\log D}>0,
\]

or any weaker bound sufficient for divergence.

A useful starting identity is

\[
\operatorname{ord}_p(10^d)
=
\frac{\operatorname{ord}_p(10)}
{\gcd(\operatorname{ord}_p(10),d)}.
\]

Averaging over \(d\) therefore converts the remaining problem into controlling
the interaction between divisors of multiplicative orders and quadratic
character sums.

## Analytic gap B: the parity / prime-values barrier

Even proving

\[
\sum_d C_d/d=\infty
\]

would still be a statement about the expected prime mass of the singular
series.  It would not by itself prove that any particular surviving value is
prime.

This is the same conceptual barrier encountered in conjectures about Mersenne
primes, Fibonacci primes and other exponentially growing sequences.

The current literature does not provide an unconditional theorem turning
local admissibility plus irreducibility plus a positive singular series into
infinitely many prime values for sequences of this growth.

## Best plausible theorem ladder

The research programme is therefore:

1. prove the universal residue-one theorem in Lean;
2. formalize the fixed-width algebra and prime-divisor consequences;
3. establish a rigorous average lower bound for \(C_d\);
4. formulate an A053067-specific period-corrected prime-value conjecture;
5. prove conditionally that this conjecture gives infinitely many prime terms;
6. continue the computational PRP search in parallel, since an actual next
   prime would settle the original OEIS existence question immediately.

The unconditional results at stages 1--3 remain worthwhile even if stage 5
ultimately requires a conjectural prime-values input.
